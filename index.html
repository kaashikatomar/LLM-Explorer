Module 1 of 6

Introduction to Artificial Intelligence & LLMs

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the hierarchical relationship between AI, Machine Learning, Deep Learning, and LLMs.
  • Learn how LLMs differ from traditional deterministic computer programs.
  • Discover what ChatGPT and foundation models actually are under the hood.
  • Identify real-world applications and the core concept of the "Next-Word Prediction" paradigm.

1. What is Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, and Deep Learning?

People often use these terms interchangeably, but they actually sit inside each other like Russian nesting dolls:

🌐 Artificial Intelligence (AI): The broadest umbrella concept. Any technique that enables computers to mimic human intelligence—including simple rule-based if/else statements.

↳ 🤖 Machine Learning (ML): A subset of AI where systems learn patterns from historical data instead of requiring explicitly hardcoded rules.

↳ 🧠 Deep Learning (DL): A subset of ML that uses multi-layered Artificial Neural Networks inspired by the human brain to process complex unstructured data (images, audio, text).

↳ 📄 Large Language Models (LLMs): Giant deep learning neural networks (built using the Transformer architecture) trained on vast internet datasets to understand and generate natural language.

2. How Are LLMs Different from Traditional Computer Programs?

In traditional software engineering (like a calculator, banking app, or tax software), a programmer writes exact instructions:
IF customer_balance >= amount THEN approve_transaction() ELSE deny_transaction(). Traditional code is deterministic: the exact same input always yields the exact same output according to rigid human rules.

LLMs do not have human-written rules for English grammar, humor, or reasoning. Instead, an LLM contains billions of tunable numbers called parameters (or weights). During training, the model reads billions of sentences and automatically adjusts its parameters until it can reliably guess what word comes next in any context.

💡 Everyday Analogy: Traditional programming is like following a strict recipe in a cookbook. An LLM is like a master chef who has tasted millions of dishes and intuitively knows which ingredients complement each other without reading a recipe.

3. What is ChatGPT?

ChatGPT is an interactive chat application created by OpenAI. It is powered by a family of foundation models (such as GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and GPT-4o). "GPT" stands for:

  • Generative: It generates new text rather than simply looking up pre-existing documents.
  • Pre-trained: It underwent massive pre-training on hundreds of billions of words from books, articles, and websites before being fine-tuned for conversation.
  • Transformer: It uses the Transformer neural network architecture (first introduced by Google researchers in 2017).

5. Important Terminology

Parameters (Weights)

The internal mathematical dial settings (billions of numbers) learned during training that determine how the model processes text.

Training vs. Inference

Training is the expensive phase where the model learns from data. Inference is when you ask it a question and it generates a response.

Hallucination

When an LLM produces an answer that sounds confident and grammatically fluent, but is factually false or made up.

Context Window

The maximum amount of text (measured in tokens) the model can remember at one time during a conversation.

Module 2 of 6

Tokenization & How LLMs Process Text

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why computers cannot read raw text directly and must convert strings into numbers.
  • Learn the difference between character-level, word-level, and subword-level tokenization.
  • Understand Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) and how unique Token IDs represent vocabulary items.
  • Discover why tokenization causes quirks in LLMs (e.g. spelling challenges and arithmetic difficulties).

1. What is Tokenization?

Computers only understand mathematics and numbers. They have no innate understanding of what the letter "A" or the word "Apple" means. Before a language model can process a single sentence, the text must be translated into a sequence of numeric integers.

Tokenization is the process of breaking down a stream of text into smaller chunks called tokens, and assigning each unique token a specific number called its Token ID.

💡 Everyday Analogy: Think of tokenization like an airport luggage check-in. The airline doesn't handle your loose sweaters and shoes individually; they pack your items into standardized suitcases and slap a unique numeric barcode tag onto each bag.

2. Why Subword Tokenization (Byte Pair Encoding)?

Early natural language systems tried two extremes:

  • Word-level: Split by spaces. Problem: The English language has over a million words, and typos or new words like "crypto" or "unfriend" break the system.
  • Character-level: Split into individual letters ('c', 'a', 't'). Problem: Sentences become thousands of characters long, making it too slow and memory-heavy for neural networks.

Modern LLMs use a hybrid approach called Subword Tokenization (specifically Byte Pair Encoding or BPE): Common words like "the", "cat", or "planet" remain single tokens. Rare, compound, or complex words are split into frequent subword chunks (e.g. "unbelievable" becomes ["un", "believ", "able"]).

Module 3 of 6

Embeddings & Vector Representations

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand what a vector is in high-dimensional mathematical space.
  • Learn how embeddings represent the semantic meaning of words as lists of numbers.
  • Understand how geometric distance translates into conceptual similarity (Cosine Similarity).
  • Discover vector arithmetic (e.g. King - Man + Woman = Queen).

1. What is an Embedding?

In Module 2, we saw that Token IDs turn words into arbitrary integers (like "cat" = 421, "dog" = 890). However, the number 421 has no mathematical relationship to 890. If we just used IDs, the computer wouldn't know that cats and dogs are both furry pets!

An Embedding solves this by converting each token into an array of hundreds or thousands of decimal numbers called a Vector. Each position in the vector represents an abstract conceptual dimension learned by the model (such as animacy, royalty, gender, size, or technological relevance).

💡 Everyday Analogy: Think of an address GPS coordinate: (Latitude, Longitude). Houses located on the same street have very close GPS numbers. An embedding is simply a GPS coordinate in a 1,536-dimensional "meaning map"!

2. Famous Vector Arithmetic: King - Man + Woman = Queen

Because meanings are mapped into geometry, you can perform actual math on word concepts:

Vector("King") - Vector("Man") + Vector("Woman") ≈ Vector("Queen")

When you subtract the "male" attribute from "King" and add the "female" attribute, the closest coordinate in the vector space is "Queen"!

Module 4 of 6

Attention Mechanisms & Transformers

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand why word meaning changes depending on context.
  • Learn why earlier AI models (RNNs/LSTMs) failed with long text passages.
  • Understand the Self-Attention mechanism: Query (Q), Key (K), and Value (V).
  • Discover the 2017 landmark paper: "Attention Is All You Need".

1. The Problem with Static Embeddings: Context Matters!

In Module 3, we saw that a word has an embedding vector. But what happens with words that have multiple meanings?

Sentence A: "I deposited money into my bank account." (Financial institution)

Sentence B: "We sat by the muddy river bank." (Sloped edge of water)

The word "bank" has completely different meanings! A model cannot just use one fixed vector. It must look at the surrounding words to update the meaning. This is what Attention does!

2. How Attention Works: Query, Key, and Value

The self-attention mechanism acts like an intelligent database retrieval system using three vectors for every word:

  • Query (Q): "What kind of context am I looking for?" (e.g. The word "it" asking: "What noun do I refer to?")
  • Key (K): "What kind of information do I offer?" (e.g. The word "animal" advertising: "I am a living creature.")
  • Value (V): "Here is my actual content and meaning to incorporate."
💡 YouTube Search Analogy: When you type a search term on YouTube, that is your Query. YouTube compares your query against the titles and tags (Keys) of millions of videos. The videos with the highest match score are chosen, and you watch the actual video content (Value).

Module 5 of 6

Next-Token Prediction & Probability

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the autoregressive loop: generating text one token at a time.
  • Learn what raw unnormalized scores (Logits) are.
  • Understand the Softmax mathematical function and how it converts logits into probabilities.
  • Learn how the model picks the next word from a probability distribution.

1. The Autoregressive Generation Loop

When ChatGPT answers your question, it does not produce the whole paragraph at once. It generates one token at a time:

  1. The model takes your prompt as input.
  2. It predicts the single most appropriate next token.
  3. It appends that new token to the prompt.
  4. It feeds the extended text back into itself to predict the next token.
  5. This repeats until the model generates a special <|endoftext|> token.

2. From Logits to Probabilities via Softmax

Before picking a word, the final layer of the transformer outputs a list of raw numeric scores called logits for every token in its vocabulary (e.g. 50,000+ words). Some logits might be 14.2, -3.5, or 8.1.

To turn these raw numbers into usable percentages that add up to 100%, the model uses the Softmax function:

Probability(Token_i) = e^(Logit_i) / Σ e^(Logit_j)

Now the model has an exact probability distribution (e.g. "sunny": 65%, "cloudy": 25%, "rainy": 8%, "purple": 0.001%).

Module 6 of 6

Temperature, Sampling & Text Generation

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand the Temperature hyperparameter ($T$) and how it scales logits.
  • Compare Greedy Decoding ($T=0$) with Stochastic Sampling.
  • Learn what happens when temperature is too low (repetitive) vs too high (incoherent).
  • Discover when to choose specific temperature settings in real AI applications.

1. What is Temperature?

Once an LLM calculates the probability of each next word, how does it choose one? If it ALWAYS picks the #1 highest-probability word every time, that is called Greedy Decoding. While safe, greedy decoding often leads to boring, rigid, and endlessly repetitive text loops.

Temperature ($T$) is a setting (usually between 0.0 and 2.0) that controls how "bold" or "creative" the model is allowed to be when rolling the dice:

  • Low Temperature ($T \approx 0.0 - 0.2$): Sharpens the probabilities. The top choice gets nearly 100% of the vote. Perfect for coding, math, and factual queries.
  • Moderate Temperature ($T \approx 0.7$): The balanced sweet spot used by default in ChatGPT for natural, fluent conversation.
  • High Temperature ($T \ge 1.2$): Flattens the distribution. Rare words get a higher chance of being picked. Great for poetry or brainstorming, but risks hallucinations and nonsense.

2. The Math: Dividing Logits by Temperature

The math is surprisingly simple: Before calculating Softmax, every logit is divided by the temperature value $T$:

Scaled_Logit = Logit / T

When $T < 1$, dividing by a small decimal makes differences between numbers much bigger (amplifying the top winner). When $T > 1$, dividing makes all numbers closer together (leveling the playing field for less likely words).
